# Signature: pressure test and round two

Round one of Signature was checked against real screens, then tightened. This page records what we looked at, what we took and what changed.

## How we looked

Screens came from Refero, pulled by search on September 30, 2026. Contact sheets are in `brand/kit/references/`. Refero's index is mostly commercial sites. It has strong editorial media and very few true nonprofits. So the second set leans on mission-led companies and impact reports. A web research pass filled the gap for civic brands we could not see on Refero. Those notes are marked as research, not screens.

We borrowed principles. We did not copy any brand's look.

**Set 1, editorial and creator-led media (Refero screens):** The New Yorker, The New York Times business section, Esquire, Bloomberg Businessweek, The Washington Post, Seed's Cultured, The Athletic's podcast page, Mailchimp Presents, The Leap and Wealthsimple Magazine.

**Set 2, quiet and mission-led (Refero screens):** Kickstarter Forward Funds, Anthropic's company page, Dopper's impact report, Duolingo's efficacy page, Nextdoor for public agencies, Mailchimp Give Where You Live, Aesop's sustainability page and Handshake's newsroom.

**Research only, not seen as screens:** Rest of World, The 19th, Semafor, Texas Tribune, ProPublica, Pew Research Center, Chalkbeat, Wikimedia Foundation, USAFacts and Code for America.

## What we saw

| What we saw | Where | What Signature does with it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Covers in a series vary, but the title and brand tag sit in the same place every time | The Athletic podcast grid | Fixed slots. One credit line top left, one headline low left. Episode numbers come off the covers. |
| The byline has one place and one style | The New Yorker, The New York Times | Name and town are one plain line, in the same spot on every surface. |
| One accent with one job | The New Yorker's labels, Anthropic's single dark button, Rest of World's written rules for its blue (research) | Marigold has two jobs: the dot, and the ninth field. Buttons and labels lose it. |
| Premium comes from space and hairlines | Wealthsimple Magazine, Anthropic, Aesop | Hairline under the byline. Plain labels. No boxes around text. |
| Contact or disclosure sits right under the title | Handshake's newsroom; Texas Tribune's fixed disclosure line (research) | The disclosure sits under the masthead on Substack and under the nav on the site, and in the footer. The wording never changes. |
| Few weights | Wikimedia uses two weights of each face (research) | Bricolage ExtraBold only. Inter Regular and SemiBold only. |
| Big, tight, low-left sans on a full field is common | Dopper's impact report, Bloomberg Businessweek | The anchor alone is not ownable. The dot, the column order and the credit line have to carry it together. |
| Trend shapes, mascots and hand lettering | The Leap, Duolingo, Mailchimp Give Where You Live | Kept out. They date fast and read as marketing. |

## Look-alike risks

- **i-D** builds its logo on the dot of an i that winks (research). Our dot stays round, flat and still. It never winks, bounces or changes shape.
- **Semafor and Mailchimp** use big yellow fields. Marigold stays scarce: one field in nine.
- **The 19th** ends its wordmark with a colored asterisk (research). Our dot stays a plain circle in one color.
- **Vox** uses a yellow highlighter sweep in video. We never highlight words in Marigold.
- **Dopper and Businessweek** use heavy low-left sans headlines. We keep fields flat and calm, with no photo cutouts or pattern behind the type.

## The five rules, now measurable

1. **One idea per frame.** A cover carries one headline of four words or fewer and one credit line.
2. **Big, tight type anchored low left.** Bricolage ExtraBold, line height 0.9, tracking minus 3 percent, three lines at most. The margin is one fifteenth of the frame width: 72 px on a 1080 px frame.
3. **Full color fields in a fixed order.** Spruce, Birch, Pine, then again. Every ninth post, Marigold takes Pine's place. In a three-across grid the colors fall into columns.
4. **Marigold once per frame, outside the wordmark.** It is the dot that ends the headline, or it is the field. On a Marigold field the dot turns Pine.
5. **Name and town are one plain line.** Name in Inter SemiBold, town in Inter Regular, sentence case. In video it sits on the safe line, 30 percent up from the bottom, clear of the app's captions.

The bug inside videos is now the wordmark alone, so there is one lockup to learn.

## Against the eight criteria

| Criterion | Round one | Round two |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Signs, does not cover | Pass | Pass. The wordmark bug is a third of the frame width, top left, and nothing else. |
| 2 Survives the crop | Pass | Pass. The avatar is unchanged. |
| 3 Carries a person | Pass | Pass, and stronger. Every cover names the creator and the town. |
| 4 Neutral | Pass | Pass. No device, no claim, no red or blue. |

# Generation Maine: Brand strategy

## Positioning statement

For young people in Maine and the people who follow them online, Generation Maine is a set of short videos and a Substack made by 8 to 12 young Maine creators about how economic rules shape their lives. Most youth policy media is national, partisan or produced by adults. Generation Maine is local, made by the creators themselves, and open about who funds it.

## Brand promise

You will hear young Mainers describe their own lives in their own words, and you will always know who is behind the project.

## Personality traits

| Trait | This | Not that |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local | Names real towns, real rents, real commutes | Talks about "America" or "Gen Z" in general |
| Straightforward | Says what happened and what it cost | Hype, slogans or talking points |
| Curious | Asks why a rule works the way it does | Tells the viewer what to think about it |
| Warm | Sounds like a friend explaining their week | Sounds like a campaign or a classroom |

**The open space:** no one owns short, youth-made video about daily economic life in Maine. The national outlets are not local. The Maine outlets are text-first and written for adults. Generation Maine can own "young Mainers on building a life here."

## Brand story

A lot of young people grow up in Maine and then face a hard question: can I afford to stay? The answer depends on rent, wages, permits, licenses and a long list of rules most people never see. Generation Maine hands the camera to 8 to 12 young creators from across the state. They film what those rules look like in their own lives, like looking for an apartment, starting a side business or deciding whether to move away. Maine Policy Institute supports the project and says so plainly. The creators tell the stories.

## Credibility stance

The likely criticism is "this is a think tank dressed up as youth content." The brand answers with two moves.

**1. Radical transparency.** Maine Policy Institute is never hidden.
- The line "An initiative of Maine Policy Institute" appears near the top of the website, in its own section and in the footer.
- The same line appears on every video end card and in every channel bio.
- It is set in the brand's plain body font at a readable size. It is never tucked into fine print or tinted to disappear.

**2. Creator ownership.** The creators' faces and voices lead.
- Photos of real creators in real Maine places are the main image on the site and in thumbnails.
- Creators appear with their own name and hometown on every lower third.
- The Generation Maine logo is small on creator content. It signs the work. It does not cover it.
- The page describes what the project does. It does not argue policy positions.

## Guardrails

- Maine-specific and youth-made. Not national, not partisan.
- Avoid founding-era Americana, flags, stars, eagles, heavy red-and-blue pairings, stock corporate photos, gradient-heavy startup style, and literal lobsters or lighthouses.
- Design for the small screen first. The mark must work as a 32 px avatar and a 9:16 end card before it works on the website.

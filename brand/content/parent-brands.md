## Maine Policy Institute, from the live site (October 2026)

Pulled from the theme's presets and a homepage screenshot. Screenshots and the logo file are in `reference/mpi/` for study only.

- **Color.** Primary royal blue #0556A5. Dark navy #0F2E4D for the top bar, text and dark panels. Light blue #2191FF. Accent yellow #FAC800 for buttons, the second headline line, and labels, with a pale #FFF7D9 tint. Muted grey #6C7781. Pages are white (#FFFFFF, with a very pale cream #FAFAE1 in places).
- **Type.** Headings: Clash Grotesk (a geometric grotesk, set heavy). Body: Red Hat Display. Alternate headings: Antarctican Mono. Labels and navigation are uppercase with wide letterspacing. Headlines are title case and bold, often with the second line in yellow.
- **Mark.** Four slanted stripes that read as forward slashes, three blue and one yellow, leaning right and converging at the base. The "//" slash recurs as a kicker device before subheads.
- **Layout and parts.** Flat rectangular buttons in yellow with navy uppercase text. A full-width royal blue hero with a navy sign-up band. Hard corners everywhere; no pills. Stock and generated photography with a bold "BLOG POST" tag. Dense, confident, institutional.

## The Maine Wire, from the live site

A news site, so the loosest of the family. Screenshots and the logo are in `reference/wire/`.

- **Color.** Near-black navy #071326 and #102747 for the masthead band, yellow #FFCC00 as the one accent, white pages, a royal blue #0B5FB8 for category tags and links. Grey rules.
- **Type.** Futura PT for the masthead and headlines (via Adobe Fonts), Lato for navigation and buttons in uppercase with letterspacing, Hind for article titles. Three sans faces, which is the sprawl of a news theme rather than a design decision.
- **Mark.** An "MW" monogram, a blue M and a yellow W interlocked between yellow and blue bars, beside "MAINE WIRE" in a heavy extended sans, "MAINE" in yellow and "WIRE" in blue. Reads as a cable news masthead.
- **Layout.** Dense card grid, black top band with a trending ticker, uppercase navigation on a white bar, blue "NEWS" tags, a boxed newsletter sign-up in the sidebar.

## Maine Civic Action, from the live site

The closest sibling to what Generation Maine needs to be, and the most useful reference. Screenshots and the logo are in `reference/mca/`.

- **Color.** Navy #112337 for the top bar and dark panels, deep blue #002F4F and #006CB5 as primaries, a lighter #338DCB, and a coral accent #F1734E (with #F59779) for every button. Off-white #FFF5F2 pages. Text #181821, muted #6C7781.
- **Type.** Headings: Gotham, heavy, title case. Body and buttons: Bricolage Grotesque. Alternate headings: Antarctican Mono. Navigation and buttons uppercase with letterspacing. Bricolage Grotesque is the same face the Signature concept uses for its display type, which means Signature is already closer to the family than it looked.
- **Mark.** A square outline in coral, open at the bottom left, with the name stacked inside in a light geometric sans: "MAINE" and "ACTION" in coral, "CIVIC" in blue. A typographic mark in a frame.
- **Layout.** Same skeleton as Maine Policy: dark top bar, hero with a sign-up band, three-column "what we do" with icons, a small boxed section label with a rule running off to the right, hard corners, flat buttons.

## The family, read across three sites

- A dark navy top bar on every site. A deep or royal blue as the primary. White or off-white pages.
- One warm accent per property: yellow for Maine Policy and the Wire, coral for Civic Action. The accent carries every button.
- Heavy geometric sans headlines (Clash Grotesk, Gotham, Futura), uppercase letterspaced navigation and labels, flat rectangular buttons, hard corners.
- Each property has its own mark, but they are all typographic or stripe based. None uses a pictorial symbol.
- The same theme skeleton: dark bar, hero, sign-up band, three columns, boxed section labels.

So "a similar look" most likely means: navy top bar, a blue primary, one warm accent of our own, a heavy geometric sans for headlines with uppercase labels, flat buttons, and a typographic mark. Within that, Generation Maine's differences can be the accent color, sentence case or lowercase headlines, and real photography of young people.

## What this means for Generation Maine

Both concepts were built to stand apart from the parent. Signature is green and marigold; Bark & Sky is quiet brown and steel blue with a lowercase serif. Neither reads as a sibling of a royal blue, yellow, heavy grotesk, uppercase system.

Options, from closest to furthest from the family:

1. **A sibling.** The family skeleton (navy top bar, blue primary, flat buttons, hard corners, uppercase labels) with Bricolage Grotesque for headlines, which is OFL and already in the Civic Action system and in Signature. One warm accent of Generation Maine's own, since yellow and coral are taken: a candidate is the Signature marigold #EFB443, which sits between the two and keeps continuity with the first concept. Photography of young people. A typographic mark in the family's manner. Cheapest to build and easiest for the executive team to approve.
2. **A cousin.** Keep MPI's navy as the anchor and one of its two accents (yellow), swap the grotesk for something younger, and drop the uppercase labels. Related, recognizably younger.
3. **Keep a current concept and add an endorsement.** Carry one of the two existing directions and tie it to the family only through the "an initiative of Maine Policy Institute" lockup and the slash kicker. Most distinct; most likely to be read as off-brand by the client.

Recommendation: build option 1 as a third concept, with the page cut down to Garrick's three jobs (what it is, what it hopes to achieve, where to find it). Hold the two existing concepts as alternatives, not as the lead.

Guardrail note: the original brief said no heavy red and blue Americana. MPI's blue and yellow is a corporate palette rather than a flag, so following the family does not break that rule, but the Americana line should still be held: no stars, no eagles, no red.

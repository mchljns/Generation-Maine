# Photography for Bark & Sky: the shot list

The page has three photo slots. The files in `brand/identity/splash-bark-sky/media/` are placeholders built from small generated thumbnails and are tagged as such. Replace each with a licensed photograph of the same shape, and the page needs no other change.

The tone, in one line: young people in Maine doing things, in daylight, with each other. Not alone, not in fog, not outside a closed mill. Understated, which means the picture is of the thing, not of the mood.

| Slot | File | Shape | What to look for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hero | `photo-hero.jpg` | 4 by 5, at least 1200 wide | Two or three people in their twenties, outdoors, mid-conversation or mid-task: a tailgate, a boat ramp, a trailhead parking lot, a shop door. Natural light, candid, nobody looking at the camera. Clothes they own, no styling |
| Creators strip | `portrait-1.jpg` to `portrait-9.jpg` | 3 by 4, at least 600 wide | The real creators once cast. Until then, nine portraits of young people in ordinary places, one per town if possible |
| Spare wide | `photo-hero-wide.jpg` | 3 by 2 | A second scene for the newsletter or follow section if the client wants one. A kitchen table with a laptop and a lease, hands in frame, morning light. Or a main street with people in it |

Rules that apply to every photograph:

- No logos, no readable signage, no flags.
- No lobsters, lighthouses, moose or lupines. The place shows in the light and the buildings, not in a symbol.
- Faces in the hero and the strip should be people who could be the creators: 18 to 29, from here. Stock that reads as a catalog model fails the test.
- Color as shot, graded a touch cooler. No duotone, no heavy filter.
- Credit the photographer where the license asks. Keep the license file beside the image in `brand/content/photos/`.

Where to license: Unsplash+ and Pexels for a fast first pass, Stocksy for documentary-looking people, the Maine Office of Tourism's media library if the project qualifies. [CONFIRM: budget and whether tourism-office imagery is open to a policy institute's project]

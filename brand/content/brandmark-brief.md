# Brand mark brief: Generation Maine, Bark & Sky

Paste everything below the line into ChatGPT (or any image model). Ask for black and white first; color comes after the form is right.

---

## What this is

Generation Maine is a media project for young Mainers, roughly 18 to 30. Nine young people in nine Maine towns film short videos about the rules behind everyday costs: leases, licenses, permits, wages, the fine print nobody reads until it costs them. A newsletter carries the full story with the numbers. The tone is plain, specific and unhurried. No hype. Stories, not positions.

Audience: Gen Z in Maine, skewing masculine, in small towns as much as Portland. Funders and policy people see it second.

## The identity it has to live in

The direction is called Bark & Sky. It is quiet.

- Colors: Bark #26201C (a near-black brown), Sky #B9C9D3 (a steel blue-grey), Paper #F4F3EE, Mist #E4E8E6, Clay #5B544C. No other colors. No gradients.
- Type: Hedvig Letters Serif, one weight, set lowercase. The wordmark is the words "generation maine" in that serif, lowercase, either on one line or stacked as "generation" over "maine".
- Everything is one weight and one color at a time. Bark on Paper, or Sky on Bark.
- Photography: real Maine landscapes and towns, graded cool and calm.

The mark will sit beside that lowercase serif wordmark, so it must be quiet enough to belong next to it and strong enough to stand alone as a social avatar.

## What the mark must do

- Read clearly at 16 pixels (a browser tab) and at 40 pixels (a comment avatar). Design it at that size first.
- Work as a single flat color with no outline, no gradient, no shading, no texture.
- Work inside a circle (avatar) and as a free shape beside the wordmark.
- Be one or two shapes. If it needs more than two shapes, it is an illustration, not a mark.
- Mean something about Maine, or about a generation finding its footing there, without being a picture of a Maine cliché.

## Do not

- No lobsters, lighthouses, pine trees, moose, lupines, buoys, boats, anchors, compasses or waves.
- No flags, stars, eagles, shields or anything that reads as patriotic or governmental.
- No initials or letters of any kind. No "G", no "M", no "GM".
- No numbers. Not 207.
- No stacks or piles of anything. Stacked stones were tried and read as the poop emoji at small sizes.
- No quotation marks, speech bubbles or dialogue devices.
- No mountains, sunrises or horizons as literal pictures. Tried; they look like clip art.
- No doors, windows, keys, houses or receipts.
- Nothing rounded and tapering. Nothing that looks like an emoji. Nothing that looks like an app icon from a template.

## What we are looking for instead

Either of these two directions:

1. **Abstract and geometric.** Two or three hard-edged shapes in one color that are simply good as a form, sit calmly beside a lowercase serif, and can carry meaning over time. Think of how a mark like a square with a notch, an offset bar, or two shapes meeting at an edge can come to stand for something. The meaning can be told in one sentence afterwards; it does not have to be read instantly.
2. **Drawn by hand.** A single confident mark made with a brush or pen in one or two strokes, scanned and cleaned, with the irregular edge left in. Not a sketch of an object. A gesture that could become a signature for the brand.

Ideas that are allowed as starting points, if treated abstractly rather than pictured: a trail blaze (one painted rectangle on a tree), the line where ground meets sky, a boundary line, a mark left so the next person can find the way, first light.

## Deliver

- 12 to 20 small marks on one sheet, each inside a 16 pixel square scaled up, black on white only.
- For the three best: the mark alone, the mark in a circle, and the mark beside the words "generation maine" set in a lowercase serif.
- Flat vector-style shapes, no shading, no 3D, no texture, no color yet.

## How we will judge it

- Still clear when shrunk to 16 pixels.
- Does not look like any existing logo, emoji or app icon.
- Belongs beside a quiet lowercase serif without shouting.
- Can be explained in one sentence.
- Would look right on a sticker on a laptop in a Lewiston coffee shop and on a funder's slide.

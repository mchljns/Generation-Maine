Generation Maine · Identity v2 · September 2026

From a logo to a system.

Version 1 set the name in a nice typeface and added an orange dot (now pink). Version 2 gives each direction a real brandmark built on the outline of Maine, disciplined type with no italics, and a set of rules a small team can repeat. v1 is saved in brand/archive/v1.

A real brandmark

A symbol that means something, works at 16 px, and can move.

Disciplined type

One main family per direction, a small set of sizes, and no italics. Emphasis comes from color.

Repeatable devices

One or two graphic devices used the same way everywhere. That consistency is what reads as premium.

Recommended

Direction A, v2

First Light

The brandmark is the outline of Maine, cut out of a rising sun. The same pink disc is the red light a camera shows while it records.

Maine is known as one of the first places in the country to see the sunrise (to verify: Cadillac Mountain, part of the year). A new generation, coming up. Stories, recorded as they happen.

The brandmark

A state, a sun, a record light.

One pink disc with Maine cut out of it. The outline comes from Natural Earth public-domain map data, simplified to 94 points so the coast still reads as Maine without fine detail that would fill in at small sizes.

The cutout is transparent, so whatever sits behind the mark shows through Maine. The disc is always Lupine pink in color versions. Nothing else in the system uses a filled pink circle.

Lockups

Type

Instrument Sans

For names, headlines and everything functional. Set tight, semibold, sentence case.

No italics

Emphasis comes from color, never from slanted type. One or two words at most go in Blossom on dark backgrounds or Lupine Deep on light ones.

Instrument Sans is under the SIL Open Font License.

Other marks explored

A third option, the silhouette with a sun rising beside it, was dropped because it echoes Baxter Brewing's sun-over-Katahdin lockup. For the same reason the whole system moved from orange to Lupine pink: Baxter's orange is #F04F2C and they print an orange Maine silhouette on every can.

Applications

The horizon rule

One thin line with the sun sitting on it. It divides sections, underlines titles and ends every video. Move the dot along the line to show progress through a series.

Direction B, v2

Postmark

Every story is sent from somewhere. The brandmark is a postmark with the outline of Maine at its center. Cancellation lines run off the edge like sound waves.

It turns into a system on its own. Each creator gets a postmark with a dot on their town. At small sizes the mark switches to ME, the state's postal code and the first-person voice of every creator.

The brandmark

Posted from Maine

Ring text in Anton: GENERATION MAINE across the top, YOUNG MAINERS across the bottom. Maine in the center. A single ink color, like a real rubber stamp. At small sizes it drops the ring text and becomes a circle with ME.

Lockup and icon

Creator postmarks

One stamp per town

Town names are examples only. Each creator's postmark marks their hometown on the map, with the name and the month they joined underneath. It goes on their lower third, their profile card and their first video.

Applications

Stamp, tape, type

Three devices: the postmark, a strip of yellow label tape behind the key words, and perforated stamp edges around photos. Anton for headlines, IBM Plex Mono for everything else.

Recommendation

Lead with First Light.

It has the strongest single idea, it reads as polished next to a policy institute's name, and it stays calm enough to let the creators' faces lead. Postmark is the more playful option and has the best built-in system for featuring each creator. Its creator stamps could also live inside First Light as a campaign device.
